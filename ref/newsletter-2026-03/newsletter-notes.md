## Skate Hire & Equipment Advice

### Hire Skates (Quads)
- Competition style skates worth over £400 per pair new
- "May look a little tired but that's because they are" - heavily used but high quality
- Much better than anything in shops
- Designed to allow maximum support and balance with smooth 'rock-over' from inside to outside edges

### Buying Skates - Warning!
- **"Shop bought skates (or those from Amazon etc) are NOT artistic roller skates, even if they say they are"**
- Parents have wasted money buying what they thought were good skates, only to find child can't complete their current grade
- Shop-bought skates: "If they don't steer they are VERY limited, harder to balance on and you probably won't pass grade 3 or 4"
- Test: "Turn the skate upside down and try to steer the wheels left and right"
- Cheap second-hand skates on eBay are cheap "because you can't give them away as they are awful to progress in"
- **"Please ask me for advice about skates before you buy any"**

### Competition Skates (Quads)
- New: £300 - £450
- Second hand: £160 - £250
- Club will swap for larger or buy back (if in same condition)
- "It really is an investment if you want your child to improve"
- Example: A pupil bought competition skates last year, now on grade 6, can out-skate most children in her class, she's 9 and all other skaters in her lessons are 12+

### Inline Skates
- Wheels should spin, but not rattle
- If they don't spin and are loose then don't buy them
- Wheels should be soft and not hard plastic with no grip
- Need a soft brake, **NOT BLACK** (black is for outside use only, will mark floors)
- Can cover with Gaffer tape as workaround
- Grade 1 can be attempted on inlines or quads, but quads required for grade 2+
